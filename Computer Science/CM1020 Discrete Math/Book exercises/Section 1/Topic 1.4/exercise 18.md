18. Suppose that the domain of the propositional function P(x) consists of the integers −2, −1, 0, 1, and 2. Write out each of these propositions using disjunctions, conjunctions, and negations.

a) ∃xP(x)
b) ∀xP(x)
c) ∃x¬P(x)
d) ∀x¬P(x)
e) ¬∃xP(x)
f) ¬∀xP(x)

---

a) ∃xP(x)

The rule:

The existential quantifier (∃) means "there exists at least one.
In a finite domain this translates to a chain disjunction (the logical OR operator, represented by V)

Answer:

P(-2) v P(-1) v P(0) v P(1) v P(2)

b) ∀xP(x)

- P(-2) ∧ P(-1) ∧ P(0) ∧ P(1) ∧ P(2)

c) ∃x¬P(x)

- ¬P(-2) v ¬P(-1) v ¬P(0) v ¬P(1) v ¬P(2)

d) ∀x¬P(x)

- ¬P(-2) ∧ ¬P(-1) ∧ ¬P(0) ∧ ¬P(1) ∧ ¬P(2)

e) ¬∃xP(x)

- ¬P(-2) ∧ ¬P(-1) ∧ ¬P(0) ∧ ¬P(1) ∧ ¬P(2)

f) ¬∀xP(x)

- ¬P(-2) v ¬P(-1) v ¬P(0) v ¬P(1) v ¬P(2)
