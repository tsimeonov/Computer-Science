17. Suppose that the domain of the propositional function P(x) consists of the integers 0, 1, 2, 3, and 4. Write out each of these propositions using disjunctions, conjunctions, and negations.

a) ∃xP(x)
b) ∀xP(x)
c) ∃x¬P(x)
d) ∀x¬P(x)
e) ¬+xP(x)
f) ¬∀xP(x)

---

a) ∃xP(x)

The rule:

The existential quantifier (∃) means "there exists at least one.
In a finite domain this translates to a chain disjunction (the logical OR operator, represented by V)

The logic:

For the statement to be true, P(x) just needs to be true for 0, OR, 1, OR 2, OR 3 OR 4

The answer

P(0) V P(1) V P(2) V P (3) V P(4)

b) ∀xP(x)

c) ∃x¬P(x)

d) ∀x¬P(x)

e) ¬+xP(x)

f) ¬∀xP(x)
