### p.133, Self-assessment question 12.4 and some questions from Exercise 12.4

1. Find the limit of each of the following sequences, as k tends to infinity, if such a limit exists.

(a) $x_k=k$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

(b) $x_k=k^2$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

(c) $x_k=\frac{100}{k}$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

(d) $x_k=\frac{1}{k}$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

(e) $x_k=k+1$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

(f) $x_k=2^k$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

(g) $x_k=(\frac{1}{2})^k$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

(h) $x_k=7+\frac{3}{k^2}$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

(i) $x_k=\frac{k+1}{k}$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

Solution:

(a) $x_k=k$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

$x_1=1$
$x_2=2$
$x_3=3$

the sequence diverges (no finite limit exists) as $k \to \infty$

(b) $x_k=k^2$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

$x_1=1^2=1$
$x_2=2^2=4$
$x_3=3^2=9$

the sequence diverges (no finite limit exists) as $k \to \infty$

(c) $x_k=\frac{100}{k}$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

$x_1=\frac{100}{1}=100$

$x_2=\frac{100}{2}=50$

$x_3=\frac{100}{3}=33.33$

the sequence converges to 0

(d) $x_k=\frac{1}{k}$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

$x_1=\frac{1}{1}=1$

$x_2=\frac{1}{2}=0.5$

$x_3=\frac{1}{3}=0.33$

$x_{1000}=\frac{1}{1000}=0,001$

the sequence converges to 0

(e) $x_k=k+1$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

$x_1=1+1=2$

$x_2=2+1=3$

$x_3=3+1=4$

the sequence diverges (no finite limit exists) as $k \to \infty$

(f) $x_k=2^k$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

the sequence diverges (no finite limit exists) as $k \to \infty$

(g) $x_k=(\frac{1}{2})^k$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

$x_1=(\frac{1}{2})^1=\frac{1}{2}=0.5$

$x_2=(\frac{1}{2})^2=\frac{1}{4}=0.25$

$x_3=(\frac{1}{2})^3=\frac{1}{8}=0,125$

the sequence converges to 0

(h) $x_k=7+\frac{3}{k^2}$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

$x_1=7+\frac{3}{1^2}=7+3=10$

$x_2=7+\frac{3}{2^2}=7+0,75=7.75$

$x_3=7+\frac{3}{3^2}=7+0,3333333333=7.33333$

the sequence converges to 7

(i) $x_k=\frac{k+1}{k}$, k = 1 , 2, 3....

$x_1=\frac{1+1}{1}=\frac{2}{1}=2$

$x_2=\frac{2+1}{2}=\frac{3}{2}=1.5$

$x_3=\frac{3+1}{3}=\frac{4}{3}=1,3333333333$

the sequence converges to 1
